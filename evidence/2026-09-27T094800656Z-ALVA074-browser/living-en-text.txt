Q01a · Example preview, not yet adopted
What brings you here today?

Clarifying your overarching goal helps calibrate the scope and level of detail for your lifestyle design.

Example premise: Assumes you are establishing your primary project goal to guide the lifestyle planning process.；All dimensions and structural layouts remain schematic and subject to physical verification.

A · Moving into a new home

Outcome if chosen: Plan whole-home functional zoning and everyday circulation for your upcoming move.

Specific example: e.g., Establishing essential living room zones, entryway drop-off, and primary furniture placement from scratch.

Trade-offs: Requires coordinating broader storage capacity and move-in milestones across spaces.

B · Planning a home renovation

Outcome if chosen: Focus on spatial restructuring, utility adjustments, and functional renovations.

Specific example: e.g., Evaluating spatial reconfigurations, opening locations, and built-in joinery needs.

Trade-offs: Involves coordinating construction feasibility, utility constraints, and phasing.

C · Refreshing a single room

Outcome if chosen: Concentrate planning entirely on maximizing the comfort and usability of this one room.

Specific example: e.g., Tailoring this living room specifically for everyday reading, relaxing, and organized storage.

Trade-offs: Focuses solely on this room without restructuring whole-house circulation or adjacent spaces.

D · Restyling current home

Outcome if chosen: Refresh visual style, mood, and loose furniture while keeping existing layouts intact.

Specific example: e.g., Updating wall and floor tones, lighting layers, and loose furniture pairings.

Trade-offs: Retains the existing room boundaries and architectural layout as-is.
